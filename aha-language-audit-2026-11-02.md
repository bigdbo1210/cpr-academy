## Verdict
Course names and formats mostly match AHA's portfolio, and the site's fine print is honest about which courses earn an AHA eCard and which earn an Academy completion certificate. **Three wording problems conflict with AHA's own published rules** and should be fixed. One required text (the AHA fee disclaimer) is missing. One flagship course is missing from the catalog.

## Fix 1 — "AHA-certified instructor" (appears across the site) — PROHIBITED PHRASE
AHA's Fraud Warning page states: "The American Heart Association does not certify trainers, doctors or training courses created by other organizations," and claims of "'American Heart Association Certified,' 'American Heart Association Approved,' 'American Heart Association Compliant' or 'created by American Heart Association certified' people… are not true."
Source: https://www.heart.org/en/about-us/statements-and-policies/fraud-warning

Current site text (examples):
- Home hero: "All courses taught by an American Heart Association–certified instructor, following current AHA guidelines."
- Catalog heading: "Official American Heart Association courses. Every course is taught by an AHA-certified instructor."
- Course pages, footer, and certificate template repeat "American Heart Association–certified instructor."

**Replacement wording:** "AHA Instructor" (aligned through an authorized Training Site). E.g., hero: "All courses taught by an AHA Instructor, following current AHA guidelines."

## Fix 2 — "Official AHA courses" framing over the whole catalog
AHA does not endorse products or services (Fraud Warning, Product Endorsement), and describes its courses through authorization/alignment, not "official" branding by commercial sites. Derrick's BLS pathway IS a genuine AHA course pathway (HeartCode BLS + skills + eCard through his Training Site arrangement). His Heartsaver-format and specialty courses on the platform are Academy courses — his own online modules and assessment, taught following AHA guidelines — and the course-page fine print already says exactly that, honestly ("It is not an AHA-approved course… does not by itself earn an AHA eCard"). The catalog-level headline overstates what the fine print correctly limits.
**Replacement framing:** headline distinguishes the two: AHA BLS Provider courses (eCard) + Academy courses taught by an AHA Instructor following current AHA guidelines (completion certificate).

## Fix 3 — The AHA fee disclaimer is REQUIRED on the website and is not present
Per AHA's Program Administration Manual (restated identically by AHA Training Centers), this disclaimer must appear on promotional materials including websites where fees are charged:

> "The American Heart Association strongly promotes knowledge and proficiency in all AHA courses and has developed instructional materials for this purpose. Use of these materials in an educational course does not represent course sponsorship by the AHA. Any fees charged for such a course, except for a portion of fees needed for AHA course materials, do not represent income to the AHA."

**Action:** add verbatim to the site footer / policy page.

## Smaller name alignments
- **"BLS for Healthcare Providers"** is the older course title. AHA's current name: **"BLS Provider Course" (Basic Life Support)**. Recommend renaming for exact match. (His BLS content itself is right: HeartCode BLS online + in-person skills session → AHA BLS Provider course completion eCard, valid two years — AHA's own description.)
- **"BLS Skills Check-Off"** is a service label, not an AHA course name; AHA calls it the "hands-on skills session" for HeartCode BLS. His how-it-works text ties it correctly to HeartCode completion and the eCard pathway. Acceptable; could subtitle it "HeartCode BLS hands-on skills session."
- **"OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens"** — AHA's course is **Heartsaver® Bloodborne Pathogens**, which meets OSHA requirements when paired with site-specific instruction and yields a participation card. Derrick's version is an Academy course; the name should not read as the AHA course.
- **"CPR Instructor Course" ($600, registration closed)** — AHA has no generic CPR instructor course; instructor training is discipline-specific (BLS Instructor Essentials / Heartsaver Instructor Essentials + instructor course + monitored teaching through a Training Center, leading to an Instructor eCard). No live risk while enrollment is closed; reword or remove the listing.
- **"Get Certified" / "official certification" (home page steps)** — AHA's credential language is "course completion eCard." For Academy courses the site correctly issues a "course completion certificate"; the step heading should say "Get Your Card / Certificate" to stay inside AHA vocabulary.
- Trademark styling: AHA renders **Heartsaver®** and **HeartCode®** with ®. His site uses the names without ® — a styling nicety, low risk.

## What MATCHES AHA (verified)
- BLS blended structure and language: HeartCode® BLS online + in-person hands-on skills session → course completion eCard. His BLS blurb and 6-step pathway mirror AHA's own blended-learning definition ("eLearning… followed by a hands-on skills session" with an AHA Instructor).
- Heartsaver course names he does carry (Heartsaver CPR AED; Heartsaver Pediatric First Aid CPR AED; Heartsaver First Aid) are real AHA course names.
- eCard language on the official pathway ("official AHA BLS Provider eCard," issued through the AHA after course requirements) matches AHA's credential model.
- "Following current AHA guidelines" (no year stated) is safe wording — see new-requirements note below.
- His BLS course content already teaches 2025 Guidelines updates (five back blows alternating with abdominal thrusts for choking; infant compression technique updates — "current guidance has moved away from the two-finger method").

## New requirements (2025 AHA Guidelines for CPR and ECC)
- Released **October 22, 2025**. Interim/new materials in use from that date.
- **Instructor Update deadline was February 28, 2026** (2025 BLS Instructor Update course, per discipline taught) — a compliance item for Derrick's own file.
- **Since March 1, 2026, only 2025 Guidelines materials are current.** Any "2020 Guidelines" claim would be stale; Derrick's site states no year — safe.
- 2025 Heartsaver restructure: **Heartsaver First Aid CPR AED** now runs in three formats (classroom, blended, self-guided) and two paths — **Heartsaver Total** (meets OSHA/regulatory requirements) and **Heartsaver Basic** (does not).
- Standing requirements confirmed: instrumented feedback devices required in adult CPR skills practice/testing (since Jan 31, 2019; elevated in the 2025 Guidelines); eCards are the AHA credential (issued through the TC, target within 20 days of completion); instructor alignment runs through Atlas with a TC/TS; no separate AHA "renewal course" exists — renewals take the same Provider course.
- No 2025–2026 change found to the fee disclaimer text or the "certified/approved/compliant" prohibition (PAM is login-gated; status = no public evidence of change).

- **Direct Link** — organization-level LMS integration for delivering AHA eLearning inside an employer's own learning system, arranged with AHA business development. It is NOT an instructor product — relevant context for the Nov 3 call with Amanda Lopez: the conversation is about employer LMS delivery, and his employer-group pipeline is the asset he brings to it.
